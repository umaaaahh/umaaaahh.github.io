Chapter 1

Banaba: Paradise Lost

Our story begins on Banaba (known during the colonial as era Ocean Island) a small but beautiful island formed atop an undersea mountain.

Favoured by seabirds as a resting spot, overtime they nourished the rock forming a lush green oasis in the middle of the pacific ocean.

The Islanders were resillent finding the only source of fresh water in the islands sacred caves.

The four villages competing today on Rabi are the original decendants from the homeland of Banaba.

Chapter 2

Cursed Gift

Unbeknownst to the Banabans the land beneath their feet held one of the richest deposit of Phosphate ever discovered.

From 1900 onward, colonial mining stripped the land, leaving forests of razor-sharp pinnacles where gardens once grew.

Like the land Banaban identity was fractured.Families were separated, traditions disrupted, and a way of life forever altered.

Chapter 3

Not Our War

The Second World War reached Banaba. Japanese forces invaded the island; many died , while the surviors were exciled at force into labour camps across Micronesia.

Many would never returned.

What world forgot we carry stil. Not our war. Still our wounds.

Chapter 4

Broken Promise

After the war ended, mining resumed. Colonial authorities declared Banaba uninhabitable and promised the Banabans a new home with infrastructure and support on a new island bought in Fiji with their own phosphate royalties.

Many promises went unfulfilled.

Chapter 5

15 December 1945

Banabans arrived on Rabi, during the peak cyclone season.

In place of infrastructure they found tents and a month of food rations.

In the wet tropics, weakened by years of forced labour, many Banabans died.

Chapter 6

First commemortation

One year later, the first December 15 Cceromony was held not as a celebration, but mourning and remembrance.

Grief turned to dermination to uphold Banaban Identity.

Rebuilding began. The villages of Uma, Tabwewa, Tabiang, Buakonikai rose again, each a promise to endure.

Chapter 7

New Beginnings

The Banabans learnt to adapt. Learning new skills, Houses and schools followed. Celebration returned

Dance preserved their cultural identiy, while sports strengthened the community bonds living in a new land.

15th of Decemember now honours the past while looking towards a brighter future.

Chapter 8

80 Years Later

Today, every December 15 we celebrate.

Four villages compete with pride and the strength of those who came before.

This is not just a tournament. It’s our story written in sweat, teamwork, and unbreakable spirit.

This is why we compete. Not for glory alone, but to honour every ancestor who fought to give us this moment.
