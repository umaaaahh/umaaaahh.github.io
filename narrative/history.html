Chapter 1

Banaba Our Homeland

Our story begins on Banaba (known during the colonial era as Ocean Island) a small Island rising from an acient undersea mountain.

Favoured by seabirds as a resting place, they enriched the land, transforming the bare rock into a lush green oasis in the heart of the Pacific Ocean.

The Banaban Islanders were resilient. With no rivers or lakes, they found their only source of fresh water deep within the sacred caves.

Banaba to Rabi, four villages compete, bound by blood, history and resilience.

Chapter 2

Cursed Gift

Unbeknownst to the Banabans the land beneath their feet concealed one of the richest deposit of phosphate ever discovered.

From 1900 onward, colonial mining stripped the land, leaving forests of razor-sharp pinnacles where gardens once grew.

Like the land Banaban identity was fractured. Families were separated, traditions disrupted, and a way of life forever altered.

Chapter 3

Not Our War

The Second World War reached Banaba with devastating force. Japanese troops invaded the island, many were killed.

Surviours were forcibly exiled into labour camps across Micronesia.

Torn from their homeland, most would never return.

We carry what the world has forgotten. Not our war, yet still our wounds.

Chapter 4

The Promised Land

After the war ended, mining resumed. Colonial authorities declared Banaba uninhabitable.

They promised the people a new home with complete infrastructure and support, on a new island in Fiji.

All paid for with wealth stripped from their own land.

From our broken land, they made broken promises and left us with the cost of excile.

Chapter 5

15 December 1945

Banabans arrived on Rabi during peak cyclone season.

Instead of promised infrastructure, they found tents and a month’s worth of food rations.

In the wet tropics, weakened by years of forced labour, many Banabans died.

Rebuilding began. The villages of Uma, Tabwewa, Tabiang, Buakonikai rose again, a vow to survive, to remember, to remain.

Chapter 6

First Commemoration

One year later, the first December 15 ceromony was held not as a celebration, but mourning and remembrance.

Remembrance was born from grief, and strength carried it forward.

Chapter 7

New Beginnings

The Banabans learned to adapt. They acquired new skills, built homes and schools and slowly, celebration returned. Dance carried their history and cultural identity, while sport forged strong community bonds in their new land.

December 15: A legacy remembered, a future reclaimed.

Chapter 8

80 Years Later

Today, every December 15 we celebrate.

Four villages compete with pride and the strength of those who came before.

This is not just a tournament. It’s our story written in sweat, teamwork, and unbreakable spirit.

This is why we compete. Not for glory alone, but to honour every ancestor who fought to give us this moment.
