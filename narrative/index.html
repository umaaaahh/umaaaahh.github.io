1900 - 1940s
From 1900, British-backed mining stripped away fertile surface and ancestral land. By the 1940s, decades of extraction and Japanese occupation during WWII had made Banaba nearly uninhabitable. The soil was taken, but the people carried their culture with them.
December 15, 1945
When over 1,000 Banaban people arrived on Rabi Island, Fiji, they were refugees of war and mining, displaced from their homeland more than 2,000 kilometres away. Sport was the last thing on their minds. They carried fragments of home, faith, and memory into an uncertain future.
1946 - Today
Rabi was rebuilt in Banaba's image. Four villages rose again—each named after their original homes. Community grew through shared labour, faith, and moments of joy. Even in hardship, the will to play persisted. Today, sport celebrates 80 years of resilience.
