The Journey to Rabi

From displacement to determination. From hardship to home.

Scroll to explore ↓
Before
1900
The Banaban people lived on Ocean Island (Banaba), a small coral island in the central Pacific. Four villages—Uma, Tabwewa, Tabiang, and Buakonikai—thrived as one people.
1900
British phosphate mining began, stripping the island bare. The Banabans' homeland was sold for fertilizer on a 999-year lease they never truly understood.
1942
1945
Japanese occupation. War. Internment camps. Starvation. Many died. Survivors were scattered across the Pacific, wondering if they'd ever see home again.
Dec 15
1945
703 souls arrived on Rabi aboard the MV Triona. Tents, not houses. Cyclone season. Two months of rations. 40 elders died. But the four villages lived on.
1946
1950
The four villages were rebuilt on Rabi, each named after their original homes. From volcanic soil and determination, a new community rose.
1965
The first inter-village sports tournament. Netball. Volleyball. Rugby. Competition became celebration. Sport became survival.
1995
50 years. The diaspora returned—from Australia, New Zealand, across the Pacific. The tournament united thousands as one people.
2025
80 years of resilience. Four villages. One island. One unbreakable spirit. The tournament continues. The story lives on.
